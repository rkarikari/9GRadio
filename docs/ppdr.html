Commentary

Narrowing the channel filter discards quantization noise for gain. The amount is set by the RF tab’s Width and sample-rate controls. The RF tab’s gain controls (Gain slider, Tuner AGC, Hardware AGC) then decide where that range sits: how strong a signal can get before the converter clips, and how weak a signal still rises above the noise.

Usable range against gain

Commentary

Blue is the range your strongest signal actually gets. At low gain that signal sits far below the clip level, so most of the converter’s range is wasted, and each extra dB of gain recovers a dB. The peak is where the clip level just meets the signal; past it the capture overloads and the range collapses. Grey is the window’s capacity, which only falls at the top end, where the tuner’s own noise takes over.

Read these as ceilings

Commentary

They assume an ideal 8-bit converter, white quantization noise and one full-scale carrier; real dongles fall short. The filter only removes quantization noise. A strong neighbour that clips the converter creates intermodulation inside your channel that no filter removes. The app warns at 0.1% of samples at the rails and flags severe overload at 1%.

For CW, CWR, USB and LSB the slider sets the demodulator’s real receive filter.
